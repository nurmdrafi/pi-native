## Abort gate (D7 / R1)

The spec's hybrid (D2) moves **match + apply + write** to Rust but deliberately
leaves **diff/patch rendering in TypeScript** — the largest risk surface
(jsdiff byte-parity) stays on the original code. The abort condition is: *if
diff/write dominate and match+apply is a small fraction, STOP*.

Measured:

- Diff+patch is the **single largest phase (58.6% aggregate, 57.2% on the 1 MiB
  class)** — and it is pinned to TypeScript by D2, so Rust cannot reduce it.
- Write is 13.5% — bytes still cross the IPC boundary and land on the same
  `writeFile` semantics (R4), so no win there either.
- Match+apply is the only phase Rust owns, and it is a **minority**:
  - small/fuzzy files: **2.4–4.0%** of total — nothing to win;
  - mid files: 9.8–20.6%;
  - the 1 MiB class: 19.2%, but that is exactly where R6 (JSON-encoding
    `baseContent` + `newContent` across a process boundary) plus process spawn
    is most expensive — the piread 60 KB-blob precedent predicts the IPC cost
    offsets the match win, and the diff (57%) remains untouched.

Even a hypothetical zero-cost Rust match would remove at most ~20% of the hot
path, and only on large/multi-edit files; on the common small-file case it is
under 5%. With spawn + IPC overhead added, the expected net is neutral-to-
negative. The hypothesis ("native match+apply can beat the built-in on
local-execution wall-clock") **fails its own feasibility gate.**

## Verdict: **ABORT S8** (measured negative)

Per the plan's contingency ("Step 0 abort → no Steps 1–8; S8 recorded as a
measured negative") and MASTER §1.7 ("Rust is a strategy, not the objective;
a negative verdict is a successful sub-project"), S8 stops here. **No Rust code
was written.** Effort redirects to **S7 (orchestration/gate steering)**.

The finding is structural, not incidental: with D2 keeping diff/patch on the
exported TS helpers (correctly, to avoid jsdiff byte-parity risk), the Rust
boundary can only ever address a minority of `edit`'s cost.
